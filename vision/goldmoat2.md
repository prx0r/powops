# The Gold Moat 2: spare parts as the first transaction layer

The opportunity is more specific than importing cheap robot accessories. It's stocking the obscure mechanical, sensing and docking components that can immobilize an otherwise repairable robot, with verified compatibility and fast UK dispatch.

---

## The gap

iRobot's UK service-parts catalogue illustrates the gap. It lists 18 products, including wheel modules, side-brush modules, dustbins and cleaning heads. But its published selection is not a comprehensive catalogue of individual sensors, circuit boards, motor subassemblies, internal cables and dock components. An iRobot customer may have to contact support or use an authorized repairer for components not sold directly.

The distinction for POW is between selling a complete £30–£300 replacement assembly and supplying the exact smaller component that failed inside it. That could reduce a repairer's cost considerably, provided the part is genuinely compatible and the repair is safe.

It also points toward newer, more complicated robots. A robotic arm or domestic assistant may contain numerous actuators, encoders, cable assemblies, bearings and gearboxes. The same intelligence that identifies replacement parts can eventually help someone manufacturing their own robot.

## The overlooked opportunity: repairing modules rather than replacing modules

The Roborock S7's LiDAR assembly is a useful example. iFixit documents how to replace the complete laser distance sensor or just the motor responsible for spinning it. Separately, its cliff-sensor replacement guide requires soldering and notes that the S7 can contain different LiDAR revisions, including different connection methods.

That suggests a specialist catalogue organized around actual repairs:

| Component | Function | Why it's interesting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LiDAR rotation motors | Rotate the navigation scanner | A faulty motor may be replaceable without replacing the optical assembly |
| LiDAR drive belts | Transfer rotation from motor to scanner | Small, lightweight; exact dimensions and material must be confirmed |
| Cliff-sensor assemblies | Detect stairs and floor edges | Specialist demand from technicians; safety-critical compatibility |
| Charging contacts and springs | Connect the robot to its dock | Small, low storage cost and relatively simple mechanical construction |
| Pump tubes, seals and gaskets | Move and contain cleaning water | Potentially economical replacements for otherwise expensive docking systems |
| Gearcases and mounting brackets | Transfer motion and support motors | Opportunity for tested replacements and eventually custom fabrication |

This is where POW begins to resemble the repair-parts infrastructure rather than an ordinary ecommerce site.

The critical feature would be part-level repair documentation: exact component, compatible machine revisions, replacement instructions, required tools and the evidence that a particular substitute works.

Don't certify cliff sensors, LiDAR optics or other safety-relevant replacements through dimensional matching alone. Functional testing is essential.

## Which parts are easiest to import?

### Relatively straightforward
**Passive mechanical parts** — Gears, replacement belts, plastic housings, brackets, silicone hoses, seals, gaskets and ordinary fasteners.

These are generally lightweight, inexpensive to store and free of lithium-battery shipping restrictions. Choose non-hazardous materials and avoid components whose failure creates a significant safety risk.

### Additional documentation and quality checks
**Low-voltage electromechanical parts** — Small motors, pump assemblies, charging contacts, cable harnesses, solenoid valves and electronic sensors.

These can be practical imports, but electrical components may fall within UK RoHS, EMC or other product requirements depending on their design and intended use. Verify documentation with the supplier.

### Defer initially
**Batteries, mains-powered boards and complex optical assemblies** — Lithium batteries require dangerous-goods handling and evidence of applicable UN testing. Mains equipment and active laser assemblies bring additional safety and conformity considerations.

For commercial stock imported from China into Great Britain, you'll ordinarily need a GB EORI number, an appropriate customs declaration, correct commodity classifications and payment or accounting for applicable import VAT and duty. Duty depends on the specific classification; there is no universal electronics-parts rate. The £135 distance-selling VAT rules are not a general exemption for importing business inventory.

For POW, this makes small mechanical repair kits especially attractive to investigate: several inexpensive components in one shipment, separated into UK-held stock after inspection.

## What iRobot doesn't provide as a public retail service

iRobot is useful as evidence that model-specific spare parts can be sold directly, but POW shouldn't build around undercutting its genuine consumables. Its wider UK accessories catalogue already includes batteries, brushes, filters, charging bases and numerous model-specific replacement products.

The commercial capabilities POW could build beyond that model are different:

* **Cross-brand repairs:** one search across iRobot, Roborock, Dreame, Ecovacs and emerging home robots.
* **Component-level replacements:** identify and validate individual motors, gears, seals and other components inside larger assemblies.
* **Verified UK availability:** distinguish physically stocked UK items from UK-facing listings fulfilled overseas.
* **Independent repair procurement:** supply exact parts and documented alternatives to technicians across multiple brands.
* **Custom fabrication:** arrange production of suitable discontinued mechanical parts and eventually the components needed for entirely new robot designs.

These are proposed differentiators, not claims that no existing company offers them. FixPart, Spares2Repair, iFixit and specialist robotic mower dealers already cover parts of this market.

## The first POW inventory experiment

Select a narrow portfolio rather than buy a general robotics catalogue. The following is a sourcing shortlist, not an unconditional purchase order.

| Initial SKU | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Dreame `20020100005232` dock pump and valve | Request two independent supplier quotes and purchase test samples |
| Dreame `20020100009618` solenoid | Confirm the exact Gen 2 dock revision, then test a sample |
| Roborock `9.01.0248` charging contacts | Check current UK dispatch times and test an alternative |
| Roborock S7 LiDAR rotation motor | Verify electrical specifications, physical fit and actual repair success |
| Matching LiDAR belts, seals and small mechanical fittings | Establish exact specifications before ordering multiple units |

The decision to stock should depend on actual UK repair demand, not just attractive Chinese prices. Collect parts requests from independent technicians, check competing UK dispatch times and calculate landed cost plus returns. Where demand is demonstrated, hold a few units and measure whether next-day availability wins orders.

Then let POW's gardens identify subsequent products. A repeated manufacturer part number across several machines, a persistent UK stockout, a long lead time or several technicians requesting the same component should trigger investigation — not automatic purchasing.

The spare parts business is the **first transaction layer**. It's the smallest, most testable version of the flywheel. You don't need to build a consumer product platform on day one. You need to prove that verified spare-parts procurement with UK-held stock generates repeat business from repair technicians.

Every fulfilled repair teaches you which parts actually fail, which alternatives work, and which suppliers deliver. That's the outcome data that makes the graph more valuable than any scrape.

The commercial moat is a verified catalogue of difficult-to-source components paired with UK-held inventory where delivery speed genuinely matters. Every fulfilled repair produces compatibility and supplier-performance evidence, making subsequent stocking decisions more informed. That same parts graph remains usable for the longer-term AI-to-physical-robot manufacturing API.
