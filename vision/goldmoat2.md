# The Gold Moat 2: spare parts as the first transaction layer

The opportunity is more specific than importing cheap robot accessories. It's stocking the obscure mechanical, sensing and docking components that can immobilize an otherwise repairable robot, with verified compatibility and fast UK dispatch.

---

## The gap

iRobot's UK service-parts catalogue illustrates the gap. It lists 18 products, including wheel modules, side-brush modules, dustbins and cleaning heads. But its published selection is not a comprehensive catalogue of individual sensors, circuit boards, motor subassemblies, internal cables and dock components. An iRobot customer may have to contact support or use an authorized repairer for components not sold directly.

The distinction for POW is between selling a complete £30–£300 replacement assembly and supplying the exact smaller component that failed inside it. That could reduce a repairer's cost considerably, provided the part is genuinely compatible and the repair is safe.

It also points toward newer, more complicated robots. A robotic arm or domestic assistant may contain numerous actuators, encoders, cable assemblies, bearings and gearboxes. The same intelligence that identifies replacement parts can eventually help someone manufacturing their own robot.

## The overlooked opportunity: repairing modules rather than replacing modules

The Roborock S7's LiDAR assembly is a useful example. iFixit documents how to replace the complete laser distance sensor or just the motor responsible for spinning it. Separately, its cliff-sensor replacement guide requires soldering and notes that the S7 can contain different LiDAR revisions, including different connection methods.

That suggests a specialist catalogue organized around actual repairs:

| Component | Function | Why it's interesting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LiDAR rotation motors | Rotate the navigation scanner | A faulty motor may be replaceable without replacing the optical assembly |
| LiDAR drive belts | Transfer rotation from motor to scanner | Small, lightweight; exact dimensions and material must be confirmed |
| Cliff-sensor assemblies | Detect stairs and floor edges | Specialist demand from technicians; safety-critical compatibility |
| Charging contacts and springs | Connect the robot to its dock | Small, low storage cost and relatively simple mechanical construction |
| Pump tubes, seals and gaskets | Move and contain cleaning water | Potentially economical replacements for otherwise expensive docking systems |
| Gearcases and mounting brackets | Transfer motion and support motors | Opportunity for tested replacements and eventually custom fabrication |

This is where POW begins to resemble the repair-parts infrastructure rather than an ordinary ecommerce site.

The critical feature would be part-level repair documentation: exact component, compatible machine revisions, replacement instructions, required tools and the evidence that a particular substitute works.

Don't certify cliff sensors, LiDAR optics or other safety-relevant replacements through dimensional matching alone. Functional testing is essential.

## Which parts are easiest to import?

### Relatively straightforward
**Passive mechanical parts** — Gears, replacement belts, plastic housings, brackets, silicone hoses, seals, gaskets and ordinary fasteners.

These are generally lightweight, inexpensive to store and free of lithium-battery shipping restrictions. Choose non-hazardous materials and avoid components whose failure creates a significant safety risk.

### Additional documentation and quality checks
**Low-voltage electromechanical parts** — Small motors, pump assemblies, charging contacts, cable harnesses, solenoid valves and electronic sensors.

These can be practical imports, but electrical components may fall within UK RoHS, EMC or other product requirements depending on their design and intended use. Verify documentation with the supplier.

### Defer initially
**Batteries, mains-powered boards and complex optical assemblies** — Lithium batteries require dangerous-goods handling and evidence of applicable UN testing. Mains equipment and active laser assemblies bring additional safety and conformity considerations.

For commercial stock imported from China into Great Britain, you'll ordinarily need a GB EORI number, an appropriate customs declaration, correct commodity classifications and payment or accounting for applicable import VAT and duty. Duty depends on the specific classification; there is no universal electronics-parts rate. The £135 distance-selling VAT rules are not a general exemption for importing business inventory.

For POW, this makes small mechanical repair kits especially attractive to investigate: several inexpensive components in one shipment, separated into UK-held stock after inspection.

## What iRobot doesn't provide as a public retail service

iRobot is useful as evidence that model-specific spare parts can be sold directly, but POW shouldn't build around undercutting its genuine consumables. Its wider UK accessories catalogue already includes batteries, brushes, filters, charging bases and numerous model-specific replacement products.

The commercial capabilities POW could build beyond that model are different:

* **Cross-brand repairs:** one search across iRobot, Roborock, Dreame, Ecovacs and emerging home robots.
* **Component-level replacements:** identify and validate individual motors, gears, seals and other components inside larger assemblies.
* **Verified UK availability:** distinguish physically stocked UK items from UK-facing listings fulfilled overseas.
* **Independent repair procurement:** supply exact parts and documented alternatives to technicians across multiple brands.
* **Custom fabrication:** arrange production of suitable discontinued mechanical parts and eventually the components needed for entirely new robot designs.

These are proposed differentiators, not claims that no existing company offers them. FixPart, Spares2Repair, iFixit and specialist robotic mower dealers already cover parts of this market.

Select a narrow portfolio rather than buy a general robotics catalogue. The following is a sourcing shortlist, not an unconditional purchase order.

| Initial SKU | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Dreame `20020100005232` dock pump and valve | Request two independent supplier quotes and purchase test samples |
| Dreame `20020100009618` solenoid | Confirm the exact Gen 2 dock revision, then test a sample |
| Roborock `9.01.0248` charging contacts | Check current UK dispatch times and test an alternative |
| Roborock S7 LiDAR rotation motor | Verify electrical specifications, physical fit and actual repair success |
| Matching LiDAR belts, seals and small mechanical fittings | Establish exact specifications before ordering multiple units |

The decision to stock should depend on actual UK repair demand, not just attractive Chinese prices. Collect parts requests from independent technicians, check competing UK dispatch times and calculate landed cost plus returns. Where demand is demonstrated, hold a few units and measure whether next-day availability wins orders.

Then let POW's gardens identify subsequent products. A repeated manufacturer part number across several machines, a persistent UK stockout, a long lead time or several technicians requesting the same component should trigger investigation — not automatic purchasing.

# POW as the vibecoding robot backend

The way POW becomes the backend for vibecoded robots is by turning its enormous parts database into a robotics build compiler.

Imagine someone asks ChatGPT to design a little robot that waters their plants, or modifies an existing LeRobot arm to pick up objects. ChatGPT can generate code, electronics schematics, CAD files and a bill of materials. But producing a design is different from obtaining a working physical machine.

POW would bridge that gap. It would know which parts actually exist, which work together, which can be substituted, what they cost from Chinese and UK suppliers, and who can manufacture and assemble everything.

The spare-parts business and this future manufacturing API would share the same underlying asset. A motor that POW has already identified, imported and tested as a replacement for an existing robot could also become a verified component in an entirely new design.

## 1. The real product is a compatibility graph, not a parts search engine

The problem with aggregating millions of listings is that a cheap motor isn't necessarily a replacement for another cheap motor.

The official LeRobot SO-101 documentation provides an excellent real example. Its follower arm uses six STS3215 servos with 1:345 gearing. Its leader arm uses three different gear ratios depending on the joint. The motor configuration, electrical supply and communication settings also matter.

If ChatGPT designs a modified SO-101 and discovers that a servo is unavailable, POW cannot simply return another STS3215 listing.

It must answer whether a different motor has sufficient torque, the correct supply voltage, matching communications and suitable mounting geometry—and whether changing it requires modifying the printed arm components or control configuration.

That's where the proprietary data comes from.

## Three types of substitution

1. **Verified drop-in** — The replacement is tested or manufacturer-approved for the exact application. No redesign required.

2. **Compatible with modifications** — The replacement could work, but requires a new mounting bracket, different power supply, connector or control configuration.

3. **Candidate only** — The specifications look suitable, but POW has insufficient evidence of compatibility. Physical testing or engineering review is required.

Each relationship should record its evidence, exact part revision, tested application, limitations and date of verification.

The enormous advantage is that the same verified substitution serves multiple markets. It can rescue a broken consumer robot, avoid a supply bottleneck in a new robot design, or help a manufacturer reduce the cost of its next production run.

## 2. What the API would actually do

### From vibecoded design to delivered hardware

```
AI coding or robotics application
  → Design files, structured BOM, CAD, target budget and destination

POW API
  → Resolve parts
  → Validate compatibility
  → Find substitutions
  → Optimize procurement
  → Quote fabrication
  → Arrange assembly and tests

  → UK-held parts / Global manufacturers / Assembly partners
  → Complete parts kit or assembled and tested robot
```

The API should initially accept structured designs or existing robotics projects, not arbitrary text prompts. The AI application handles turning the user's idea into a design. POW handles the physical feasibility, procurement and manufacturing aspects.

For developers, expose a compact set of services:

| API | Function |
| --- | --- |
| `resolve_bom` | Turn a project's parts list into exact manufacturer and POW part identities |
| `find_substitutes` | Return evidence-backed alternatives, including required modifications |
| `optimize_bom` | Compare cost, availability, delivery time and substitution risk |
| `quote_build` | Combine component, printing, fabrication, assembly and shipping quotations |
| `create_order` | After authorization, submit orders and track production and delivery |
| `find_replacement` | Later, source repair parts for the completed robot |

These should be ordinary HTTP endpoints, exposed through MCP for compatible AI assistants.

## 3. The manufacturing infrastructure already exists

POW wouldn't need to own a factory to establish its first manufacturing service.

JLCPCB documents APIs for electronic components, PCB production and 3D printing, including quoting, ordering and tracking. Access requires approval.

Seeed Studio already accepts component BOMs, attempts to match manufacturer part numbers to live supplier prices and refers unmatched components to its procurement team.

POW's opportunity sits above these businesses. It could divide one robot design between component distributors, a 3D-printing service, a PCB assembly provider and a specialist robot assembly workshop.

However, a successful parts order is not a successfully manufactured robot. The manufacturing service would also need assembly instructions, calibration procedures, functional tests, quality control and product-safety responsibility.

That's why the first fully assembled robots should be based on supported, validated designs rather than accepting every AI-generated design immediately.

## 4. The moat: parts intelligence becomes physical distribution

Imagine POW discovers that the same exact actuator is used in several popular open-source arms and repairable commercial robots. Its global supplier garden knows the wholesale price and manufacturing lead time; its UK robot garden knows which machines use it; its repair garden knows how frequently customers request it.

That creates a much more informed inventory decision than simply importing popular electronic components.

### The accumulating advantage

One verified component, multiple sources of demand: existing robot repairs + new robot builds + repeated manufacturer orders.

POW can then hold selected parts in the UK, while sourcing slower-moving components directly from overseas. A developer building a new robot benefits from the same local inventory that a technician uses to fix an existing one.

Each actual purchase, substitution test, return and completed build then produces more evidence about which components are reliable and worth stocking.

The moat isn't the number of raw supplier listings. It's the combination of verified cross-model compatibility, historical delivered costs, accumulated repair outcomes, physical UK inventory and reliable manufacturing relationships.

An AI agent could retrieve a component's advertised specifications elsewhere. It would be much harder to replicate POW's record showing that a particular substitute worked in 40 actual builds, that another supplier repeatedly delivered defective batches, or that a specific motor assembly is available for next-day UK dispatch.

## 5. How this becomes a business

| Commercial product | Revenue mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| UK robot spare parts | Margin on stocked components and repair kits |
| BOM procurement | Fee or margin on complete component baskets |
| Custom robot manufacture | Coordination, assembly and fulfilment fees |
| Robotics API | Usage-based or subscription access for commercial applications |
| Manufacturer procurement | Repeat supply contracts and volume-based margins |

The interesting longer-term customer is another company building a consumer robotics application.

Imagine a developer launches an app where users create personalized desktop robots. That developer shouldn't need to establish Chinese supplier relationships, create a UK warehouse, verify every actuator or contract with assembly factories. Its application could use POW for quotations, sourcing, manufacturing and fulfilment while retaining its own brand and customer experience.

POW would be the infrastructure provider, just as print-on-demand platforms supply manufacturing and fulfilment for independent ecommerce businesses.

## 6. Build one real vertical slice first

Make a modified SO-101 the first reference design. Get its complete official BOM into `powproducts`, resolve its exact components and identify alternatives that are either verified or clearly marked as requiring engineering review. Import its printed-part files and mechanical specifications into the robotics catalogue.

Then have `powphysical` return two executable UK-delivered purchasing baskets: one optimized for delivery time and another for cost. Add a fabrication quotation for a modified gripper. Finally, place a supervised real order and document the actual assembly and testing results.

This exercises the future API with a real robot, without needing to develop the software or AI robotics framework yourself.

Once that works, expose it through MCP and test whether an AI assistant can successfully request, modify and obtain a complete quote for the supported design.

The immediate product would be a buildable BOM, verified substitutions and an executable UK-delivered quotation. The eventual product is a complete robot delivered to the customer's door.

That is the bridge between the current Data Gardens investment and the Printify-for-robots vision.
